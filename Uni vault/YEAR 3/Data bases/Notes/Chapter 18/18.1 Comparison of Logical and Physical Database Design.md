### **18.1 Comparison of Logical and Physical Database Design**

#### **Summary**

This section contrasts the logical and physical phases of database design.

*   **Logical Design is the "What":**
    *   **Goal:** To define the *structure* of the data (tables, columns, relationships, constraints) without considering how it will be stored.
    *   **Output:** A DBMS-independent blueprint, like an ER diagram and a relational schema.
    *   **Focus:** Data requirements and rules.

*   **Physical Design is the "How":**
    *   **Goal:** To implement the logical design efficiently on a specific DBMS and hardware.
    *   **Output:** A technical specification for implementation, including file structures, indexes, and storage details.
    *   **Focus:** Performance and storage. It is highly dependent on the specific DBMS's features.

*   **Key Differences:**
    *   They require different skills; logical designers are modelers, and physical designers are systems experts.
    *   The process is iterative. Decisions made in physical design for performance (like merging tables) can force changes back to the logical model.
