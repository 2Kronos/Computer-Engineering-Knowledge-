**🎯 Objective:**

- Ensure the **conceptual data model** is a **true and accurate representation** of the enterprise’s **real data requirements** — _from the user’s perspective_.
    

---

### 🗂️ What Happens in This Step?

✅ **Present the ER diagram** and all supporting documentation to the **users/stakeholders**.  
✅ **Explain the entities, relationships, attributes**, and how they **map to the real-world processes**.  
✅ **Listen carefully** to user feedback:

- Are there **errors, omissions, or misunderstandings**?
    
- Does the model **match how the business actually works**?  
    ✅ **Update the model** to correct any issues found.
    
- You may need to **repeat earlier steps** to fix problems.  
    ✅ **Repeat review cycles** until the user is fully satisfied and **signs off** the model.
    

---

### 📌 Key Outcome

✔️ **User sign-off** = Agreement that the model **correctly describes their data needs**.  
✔️ The **conceptual design** is now **validated** — ready for **logical database design**.

---

### ✅ Practical Tips

- Use **clear visuals** and **plain language** — avoid technical jargon.
    
- Make sure **supporting documents** (like the **data dictionary**) are clear and complete.
    
- Keep **track of revisions** so changes are transparent.
    
- Be prepared for **multiple review sessions** — it’s normal!
    

---

### 📚 What’s Next?

- Once the user signs off:
    
    - **Step 1 (Conceptual Design)** is complete.
        
    - The project moves to **Logical Database Design** (described in the next chapter).
        

---

## ✔️ Summary

- This step ensures that the data model is **correct, complete, and trusted** by the people who use it.
    
- A good review prevents expensive mistakes in **later design and implementation**.
