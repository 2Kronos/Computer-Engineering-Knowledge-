### **20.6 Data Administration and Database Administration**

#### **Summary**

This section distinguishes between two key organizational roles responsible for data management: **Data Administration (DA)** and **Database Administration (DBA)**. They are often separate functions but may be combined in some organizations.

*   **Focus:** Manages the **data itself** as a corporate resource. This includes both computerized and non-computerized data.
*   **Scope:** Strategic and business-oriented. Concerned with the **early stages** of the database lifecycle: planning, analysis, and logical design.
*   **Responsibilities:** Defining data standards, policies, and procedures. Creating the corporate data model, determining data requirements, and ensuring data strategies align with business goals. Developing the overall security policy.
*   **Orientation:** **Managerial and business-focused.** Acts as a consultant to senior management.

*   **Focus:** Manages the **database system** and its physical implementation.
*   **Scope:** Technical and operational. Concerned with the **later stages** of the lifecycle: physical design, implementation, and maintenance.
*   **Responsibilities:** Selecting and installing the DBMS, physical database design, implementing security/integrity controls, performance tuning, backup and recovery, and daily operational maintenance.
*   **Orientation:** **Technical and DBMS-specific.** Requires deep knowledge of specific database software and operating systems.

**Key Difference:** DA is concerned with **what** data is stored and **why** from a business perspective (DBMS-independent). DBA is concerned with **how** the data is stored and accessed efficiently and securely (DBMS-dependent). DA is strategic; DBA is tactical and operational.
