
### 📘 Concept Definition

> **Database design** is the process of creating a design supporting the enterprise’s mission and objectives.

It translates requirements into a blueprint for implementation.

---
### 10.6.1 Approaches to Database Design

#### **1. Bottom-Up Approach**

- Starts with **attributes** → groups them into **relations** (normalization).
    
- **Best for**: Simple databases with few attributes.
    
- **Challenges**: Hard to scale for complex systems (e.g., 1000s of attributes).
    

#### **2. Top-Down Approach**

- Begins with **high-level entities/relationships** → refines into attributes.
    
- Uses **Entity-Relationship (ER) modeling** (e.g., `PrivateOwner` → `PropertyForRent`).
    
- **Best for**: Complex databases (e.g., enterprise systems).
    

#### **Other Approaches**:

- **Inside-Out**: Starts with major entities, then expands.
    
- **Mixed Strategy**: Combines top-down and bottom-up.
---

### 10.6.2 Data Modeling

**Purpose:**

- Understand data semantics (meaning).
    
- Communicate requirements between users/designers.
    

Data modeling answers:

- **Which entities** matter?
    
- **How they relate?**
    
- **What attributes** they have?
    

**Criteria for an Optimal Data Model** (Table 10.2):

- **Structural validity:** Matches real-world structure
    
- **Simplicity:** Easy to understand
    
- **Expressibility:** Captures all needed constructs
    
- **Nonredundancy:** No duplicate representation
    
- **Shareability:** Usable across applications
    
- **Extensibility:** Adapts to new requirements
    
- **Integrity:** Enforces business rules
    
- **Diagrammatic representation:** Clear visual notation
    

> ⚖️ Trade-offs are often needed (e.g., more expressibility vs. simplicity).

### 10.6.3 Phases of Database Design

Database design proceeds in **three phases**, each feeding the next:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[Conceptual Design] --> B[Logical Design]
    B --> C[Physical Design]
```

1. **Conceptual Design**
    
    - Build an **ER/EER model** of the enterprise, independent of any DBMS.
        
    - Focus on **entities**, **relationships**, **attributes**, and **constraints**.
        
    - Validate against user requirements.
        
2. **Logical Design**
    
    - **Map** the conceptual model onto a **target data model** (e.g., relational).
        
    - Define **schemas**, **keys**, and **integrity constraints**.
        
    - Use **normalization** to eliminate redundancy.
        
    - Still **DBMS-independent**, ignoring physical storage details.
        
3. **Physical Design**
    
    - Tailor the logical design to a **specific DBMS** (relational, object-oriented, etc.).
        
    - Choose **storage structures**, **indexes**, and **access methods**.
        
    - Define **security** and **backup** strategies.
        
    - Refine logical schema if needed for performance.
        

---

### 📖 Mapping to ANSI-SPARC Architecture

- **External (User Views) ↔ Conceptual Model**
    
- **Conceptual (Unified View) ↔ Logical Model**
    
- **Internal (Storage) ↔ Physical Model**
    

---

> **Tip:** Keep conceptual/logical design separate from physical to focus on **“what”**, before deciding **“how.”** Iteration between phases is normal as you learn more about requirements and performance needs.
