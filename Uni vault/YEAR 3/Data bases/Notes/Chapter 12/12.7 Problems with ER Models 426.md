



## ⚠️ 12.7 Problems with ER Models

When designing ER models, **connection traps** can occur due to incorrect or unclear relationships between entities. These traps lead to **ambiguous** or **missing paths** in the data model, making it impossible to answer certain queries.

There are two main types of connection traps:

- **Fan traps**
    
- **Chasm traps**
    

Understanding and avoiding these traps is essential for building accurate models that truly represent the real-world structure.

---

## 🔄 12.7.1 Fan Traps

### 📘 Definition:

A **fan trap** occurs when:

> A model contains 1:* relationships that “fan out” from the same entity, but the **pathway between related entities becomes ambiguous**.

---

### 🧠 Example Scenario:

![`Pasted image 20250526203811.png`](images/`Pasted image 20250526203811.png`)

Entities involved:

- `Division`
    
- `Branch`
    
- `Staff`
    

Relationships:

- A `Division` **operates** multiple `Branches` (1:*)
    
- A `Division` **has** multiple `Staff` (1:*)
    

#### ❗ Problem:

This model **does not directly show** which staff work at which branches.  

> Ambiguity arises because the model implies a connection **through Division**, but **doesn’t define a direct relationship between Staff and Branch**.

---

### ✅ Solution:

![`Pasted image 20250526203858.png`](images/`Pasted image 20250526203858.png`)

- Add a direct relationship between `Staff` and `Branch` (e.g., **"WorksAt"**).
    
- This removes the ambiguity and allows accurate tracking of which staff members work at which branches.
    

---

## 🧱 12.7.2 Chasm Traps

### 📘 Definition:

A **chasm trap** occurs when:

> A model suggests a relationship **should exist** between two entities, but because of **optional participation** (minimum multiplicity of 0), the **pathway between entities breaks** in some cases.

---

### 🧠 Example Scenario:

Entities involved:

- `Branch`
    
- `Staff`
    
- `PropertyForRent`
    

Relationships:

- A `Branch` **has** one or more `Staff`
    
- `Staff` **oversee** zero or more `PropertyForRent`
    

#### ❗ Problem:

Suppose property PA14 is **not currently assigned** to a staff member.  
Then we cannot determine which branch PA14 is associated with — because the only path from `PropertyForRent` to `Branch` is **via `Staff`**, and **that link is missing**.

> This results in **a loss of information**, despite knowing that all properties must belong to a branch.

---

### ✅ Solution:
![`Pasted image 20250526204032.png`](images/`Pasted image 20250526204032.png`)

- Add a **direct relationship** between `Branch` and `PropertyForRent` (e.g., **"Offers"**).
    
- This ensures that even if a property is not yet overseen by staff, it is still known to belong to a branch.
    

This change is shown in the fixed ER model (Figure 12.22a), where the relationship between `Branch` and `PropertyForRent` is explicitly defined.

---

## 📌 Summary of Connection Traps

|Trap Type|Problem|Fix|
|---|---|---|
|**Fan Trap**|Pathway between related entities is **ambiguous** due to multiple 1:* from one source.|Add direct relationship to clarify connections.|
|**Chasm Trap**|A **missing pathway** due to optional participation (0 as min multiplicity).|Add missing relationship to preserve information flow.|

Understanding and identifying these traps helps avoid flawed ER models that could lead to incorrect or incomplete data interpretations.
