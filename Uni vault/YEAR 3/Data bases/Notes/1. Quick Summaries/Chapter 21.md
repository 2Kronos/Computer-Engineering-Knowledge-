### **21.1 Explain the motives behind organizational ethics and legislations.**

**Answer:**
- Ensure accountability, transparency, and trust
- Protect stakeholder interests (customers, investors, employees)
- Respond to corporate scandals and failures
- Establish minimum standards of behavior
- Guide behavior where laws are unclear or absent

---

### **21.2 Describe business situations in which an individual’s or businesses’ behavior would be considered: (a) illegal and unethical; (b) legal but unethical; (c) illegal but ethical.**

**Answer:**
**(a) Illegal & Unethical:** Financial fraud, insider trading, stealing customer data
**(b) Legal but Unethical:** Employee snooping in customer databases, aggressive data mining without clear consent
**(c) Illegal but Ethical:** Whistleblowing on illegal activities, refusing improper data requests to protect privacy

---

### **21.3 Explain the role of the organizational code of conduct in relation to local and international legislation.**

**Answer:**
- Translates laws into specific employee guidelines
- Sets higher standards than minimum legal requirements
- Ensures consistent behavior across international operations
- Reinforces ethical culture beyond legal compliance

---

### **21.4 Describe the importance of IT governance and its relationship to legal and ethical practice in organizations.**

**Answer:**
- Provides framework for legal compliance (SOX, HIPAA, GDPR)
- Implements controls and accountability structures
- Aligns IT with business objectives ethically
- Uses frameworks like COBIT to manage IT resources responsibly

---

### **21.5 Explain how an international business can be influenced by laws from a foreign land and how it may face liability from its customers for reasons beyond its control.**

**Answer:**
- Must comply with all countries where they operate (e.g., GDPR for EU customers)
- Subject to foreign laws like USA Patriot Act for data access
- Liability from conflicting legal requirements between jurisdictions
- Penalized for actions compelled by one country's laws that violate another's

---

### **21.6 Explain the legal risks to an international business in data management.**

**Answer:**
- Heavy fines for GDPR/HIPAA/SOX violations
- Legal conflicts between different countries' data laws
- Liability for data breaches affecting international customers
- Penalties for improper cross-border data transfers

---

### **21.7 Describe how an entity or individuals outside and within organizations can have legitimate power to access corporate data and, therefore, influence how it is managed.**

**Answer:**
**Internal:** Employees, DBAs, internal auditors, management  
**External:** Government agencies, law enforcement (with warrant), external auditors, business partners (contractual)  
**All influence** data security, retention, and access policies

---

### **21.8 Describe some of the legal challenges faced in protecting Intellectual property and promoting innovation with emerging technology.**

**Answer:**
- Laws lag behind emerging tech (AI, blockchain)
- Inconsistent software patent standards globally
- Difficulty enforcing digital IP across borders
- Complex open-source license compliance
- Defining and protecting data ownership rights
- Balancing IP protection with innovation promotion
