
This chapter explores the professional, legal, and ethical challenges faced by data and database administrators in the context of modern data management. It emphasizes the growing importance of compliance, governance, and ethical behavior due to increased regulatory scrutiny and technological complexity.

**Key Topics Covered:**

1. **Ethical vs. Legal Behavior**:  
   - Ethics involves “doing what is right” according to societal standards, while laws enforce certain ethical behaviors.  
   - Not all unethical behavior is illegal, and not all ethical behavior is legal. Examples include unauthorized data access and regulatory conflicts like SEC Reg NMS.

2. **IT Governance**:  
   - Defined as a framework for decision rights and accountability to encourage desirable behavior in IT use.  
   - Frameworks like **COBIT 5** and **COSO** help organizations align IT with business goals, manage risks, and ensure compliance.

3. **Major Legislation and Regulations**:  
   - **Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX)**: Enhances financial transparency and requires robust internal controls and auditing.  
   - **HIPAA**: Protects patient data privacy and standardizes electronic health records.  
   - **EU Data Protection Directive (1995)**: Establishes strict principles for personal data processing, including notice, consent, and security.  
   - **UK Data Protection Act (1998)**: Implements EU principles, ensuring fair and lawful data processing.  
   - **Basel II/III Accords**: Require banks to maintain capital based on risk exposure (credit, market, operational risk) and improve disclosure.  
   - **Freedom of Information Acts**: Grant public access to government-held information, promoting transparency.

4. **Developing an Ethical Culture**:  
   - Organizations should establish clear policies for legal and ethical behavior, supported by training and accountability.  
   - Professional codes of ethics (e.g., from ACM and BCS) provide guidelines for IT professionals, emphasizing public interest, competence, and integrity.

5. **Intellectual Property (IP)**:  
   - Includes patents, copyrights, and trademarks.  
   - Software is typically protected by copyright, though patentability varies by jurisdiction.  
   - Data licensing and sharing must be carefully managed to protect IP and comply with laws like the USA Patriot Act.

6. **Challenges and Best Practices**:  
   - Data administrators must balance data accessibility with security and privacy.  
   - Regular policy reviews, employee training, and adherence to frameworks like COBIT help maintain compliance and ethical standards.

**Conclusion**:  
The chapter underscores the critical role of data professionals in ensuring legal compliance, ethical data stewardship, and robust IT governance. It highlights the need for ongoing education, clear policies, and adaptive strategies to navigate the evolving legal and technological landscape.
