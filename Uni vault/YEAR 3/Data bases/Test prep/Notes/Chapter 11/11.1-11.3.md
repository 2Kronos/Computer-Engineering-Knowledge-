## Chapter 11: Database Analysis & the DreamHome Case Study

### 📘 Chapter Objectives

- Understand **when** and **how** fact-finding techniques fit into the lifecycle.
    
- Identify **types of facts** collected and **documentation** produced at each stage.
    
- Learn the **five main** fact-finding techniques, with their pros/cons.
    
- Apply these techniques to the **DreamHome** case study’s early lifecycle stages.
    

---

## 11.1 When Are Fact-Finding Techniques Used?

### 📘 Definition

> **Fact-finding** is the formal process of using interviews, questionnaires, documentation review, observation, and research to collect accurate information about systems, requirements, and user preferences.

---

### 🔍 Lifecycle Touchpoints

Fact-finding is **most critical** during:

- **Database Planning**
    
- **System Definition**
    
- **Requirements Collection & Analysis**
    

It’s also used (to a lesser extent) during:

- **Design** (e.g., exploring DBMS features)
    
- **Operational Maintenance** (e.g., performance tuning needs)

## 11.2 What Facts Are Collected?

At each stage, specific facts are captured and **documentation** produced:

|Lifecycle Stage|Facts Collected|Documentation Produced|
|---|---|---|
|**Database Planning**|Project aims, objectives|Mission statement & objectives document|
|**System Definition**|Major user-views, scope & boundaries|System definition spec, user-view descriptions|
|**Requirements Analysis**|Data needs, transactions, performance/security|Requirements specification|
|**Database Design**|Feedback on ER/logical models, DBMS features|ER diagrams, data dictionary, logical schema|
|**Application Design**|UI feedback, transaction flows|Application design spec, UI mock-ups|
|**DBMS Selection**|DBMS capabilities vs. requirements|Vendor evaluation reports|
|**Prototyping**|User reactions to prototype|Revised requirements/specs|
|**Implementation & Loading**|DDL/DML functionality, data conversion needs|Implementation scripts, conversion plan|
|**Testing**|Errors found, performance metrics|Test plans & results|
|**Operational Maintenance**|Usage stats, tuning needs, new requirements|Maintenance logs, updated requirements|

## 11.3 Fact-Finding Techniques

### 🔹 Technique 1: Examining Documentation

- **What:** Review existing memos, reports, manuals, forms, flowcharts, and data dictionaries.
    
- **Pros:** Quickly understand legacy systems and business context.
    
- **Cons:** May be outdated or incomplete.
    

### 🔹 Technique 2: Interviewing

- **What:** Structured (predefined questions) or unstructured (open discussion) one-on-one sessions.
    
- **Pros:** Rich insights, body-language cues, follow-up questions.
    
- **Cons:** Time-consuming; depends on interviewer skill and interviewee availability.
    

### 🔹 Technique 3: Observation

- **What:** Watch or participate in daily operations to see workflows and data usage.
    
- **Pros:** Validates reported facts; reveals actual practices.
    
- **Cons:** Users may change behavior when watched; may miss peak workloads.
    

### 🔹 Technique 4: Research

- **What:** Consult trade journals, books, online forums, and existing case studies.
    
- **Pros:** Leverages external solutions and standards.
    
- **Cons:** Requires good source access; problem may be unique.
    

### 🔹 Technique 5: Questionnaires

- **What:** Distribute free-format or fixed-format surveys to many users.
    
- **Pros:** Scalable, confidential responses, quick tabulation.
    
- **Cons:** Low response rates; limited follow-up on ambiguous answers.
    

---

### 📊 Benefits vs. Drawbacks

|Technique|Key Advantages|Key Disadvantages|
|---|---|---|
|Documentation|Fast, broad context|May be obsolete|
|Interviewing|Deep, flexible probing|Resource-intensive|
|Observation|Real behavior, environment details|Observer effect, scheduling issues|
|Research|Existing best practices|Not always directly applicable|
|Questionnaires|Large coverage, quantitative analysis|Poor response; lacks depth|
