
### **1. What is Quantization?**  
Quantization is the process of segmenting a sampled analog signal into a fixed number of discrete voltage levels (quantiles). Each level is assigned a numerical value, which is then converted into a binary code.  

- **Importance:** Enables the conversion of continuous analog signals into digital (binary) format, making them suitable for digital processing, storage, and transmission.  

### **2. Why is Pulse Code Modulation (PCM) important?**  
PCM is important because:  
- It provides a digital representation of analog signals.  
- It is widely used in digital audio (CDs, digital tapes), telecommunications (global telephone systems), and video processing.  
- It allows for noise-resistant signal transmission and long-distance communication through regeneration.  

### **3. What is Quantization Error or Quantization Noise?**  
Quantization error (or noise) is the difference between the original analog signal’s amplitude and the nearest quantized digital value. This occurs because the analog signal has infinite possible values, while digital systems use a finite number of quantization levels.  

### **4. Application Areas of Pulse Code Modulation (PCM)**  
- **Digital Audio Recording** (CDs, Digital Audio Tape).  
- **Telecommunications** (Global telephone networks).  
- **Digitized Video Effects** (Laser discs, digital video processing).  

### **5. What is Quantization Resolution and Dynamic Range?**  
- **Quantization Resolution:**  
  - The smallest detectable voltage change in a digital system.  
  - Formula:  
    $\text{Resolution} = \frac{\text{Full-scale voltage}}{2^{\text{(Number of bits)}}}$
  - Higher bit count → More quantization levels → Better resolution.  

- **Dynamic Range:**  
  - The ratio of the maximum to minimum voltage levels a system can represent.  
  - Signals with high dynamic range have both very large and very small amplitude values.  
  - Important for accurately representing signals like audio (soft whispers and loud sounds).  

### **6. Role of Parameters in a Quantization System**  
- **Resolution:** Determines how accurately an analog signal is digitized.  
- **Dynamic Range:** Ensures both weak and strong signals are captured without distortion.  
- **Quantization Levels:** More levels reduce quantization noise but require more bits.  
- **Linear vs. Non-linear Quantization:**  
  - **Linear:** Uniform step sizes (simpler, used in general applications).  
  - **Non-linear:** Non-uniform steps (better for high dynamic range signals like voice, uses companding).
